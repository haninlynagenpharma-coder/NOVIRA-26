Scientific interpretation

The overall analysis revealed a higher hospitalisation rate in the AVX-7 group compared with the Standard care group (30.9% vs 15.7%) still this observed difference should be interpreted with caution as the groups had major baseline clinical differences.

In addition patients in the AVX-7 group tended to be older had a higher prevalence of comorbidities lower oxygen saturation SpO₂ higher CRP levels and a much greater proportion of high risk patients(47.3% vs 3.1%) mean SpO₂ and CRP values were calculated using available measurements only because some data were missing therefore these means do not represent the entire patient cohort.

When the groups were stratified according to baseline risk the AVX-7 treatment group showed consistently lower hospitalization rates in all three categories: low risk (2.6% vs 9.1%) moderate risk (16.5% vs. 29.1%), and high risk (51.3% vs. 68.6%) this reversal in direction between the overall comparison and the stratified results displays a pattern consistent with Simpson's paradox highlighting the potential influence of confounding factors and baseline disease severity.

However stratification by baseline risk does not necessarily eliminate all confounding, as variables such as age comorbidities SpO₂ levels and CRP may still contribute to residual confounding overall these findings demonstrate how relying solely on crude hospitalization rates may provide a misleading picture when baseline characteristics are not adequately considered.

The risk stratified data indicate an observed association between receipt of AVX-7 and lower hospitalization rates within each risk category but this does not establish causality given the limitations inherent in observational study designs further multivariable statistical analyses and randomized controlled trials RCTs are needed to better characterize this association a dedicated evaluation of adverse events and safety endpoints is also required to assess the safety profile of AVX-7.
